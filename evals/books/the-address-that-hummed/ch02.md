# Chapter 2: The Box With No Bottom

After the Orchard closed its gates, Ivara returned to the Vellum Hall alone. Eren Vey was waiting there. The tin box knocked against Ivara's hip as she walked. It was lighter than a box should be, and the tapping from within followed the rhythm of her steps, half a beat late.

Tovan had locked the Mouth Room. He stood outside it with a bone comb in his hand. Its teeth were different lengths, and each tooth bore a tiny number. The comb had belonged to an earlier keeper of the Hall, who used it to comb the sounds from a new room until it could speak plainly. Tovan claimed he had borrowed it from the Hall's collection of unwanted instruments. The collection denied owning it. Under Ivara's cuff, the red key warmed; Tovan had not borrowed it.

“You brought back an unregistered seam,” he said. “And a sound that matches this room.”

“Did the Mouth Room move?” asked Ivara.

“No. It answered from the Orchard.”

Eren Vey set his notes on the table. “A room can share a voice.”

“People share a voice. Rooms share walls.” Tovan turned the comb over. “Unless they're the same room.”

At that, Ivara felt the red key stay cool in her cuff; Tovan's suggestion was true. She removed the tin box and put it on the table. The parcel's blue thread had gone slack. A small folded paper lay beneath the false bottom, tucked beside the map and thimble, though she had not opened the box.

Tovan read the note aloud: “A room may be stored by its first name, but not its walls.” The note was signed E. Vell.

“You gave this to me,” Ivara said.

“I gave you a box,” said Eren Vell from the doorway. “I didn't know there was a note.” The porter had come in without his usual stack of bark. His palms were stained with blue ink.

The two Erens stood facing each other. Eren Vey looked down at the note. “You wrote that.”

Eren Vell laughed, which made his face look less like the clerk's. “I don't write. I carry.”

The claim seemed true until Ivara saw the ink on his palms. The red key warmed: Pell was lying. Ivara picked up the box. Its inside was lined with felt, but when she tilted it, the tapping came from somewhere deeper than the base.

Tovan demanded the parcel. Ivara refused. She took the box to the Understair, with Eren Vey behind her and Eren Vell ahead. The passage was still officially sealed, though its door had been painted over and the paint had a fresh crack running through the middle. Under the stairs, the air smelled of wet paper though there was no water in Oru.

The passage narrowed into a room called the Sump, where discarded addresses were pressed into clay. In its center stood a stone basin. Ivara set the tin box inside it. The box lifted its lid by itself. A little brass map lay within, folded into a square. On its face, the map showed the Tin Orchard, the Vellum Hall, and a narrow room drawn between them. That room had no entrance. In its center was the word LATCH.

Beneath the map, they found a glass thimble containing a black thread. The thimble was capped with red wax. The tin box had looked empty because its bottom was hinged upward; the objects had been tucked in the false base.

Eren Vey leaned close. “The plan on this map is older than the Orchard,” Eren Vey said.

“Older than the Hall,” said Eren Vell. “The brass copy is newer. It shows rooms before they were divided.”

Tovan arrived carrying the bone comb. He had taken the other staircase, or perhaps the Sump had brought him to them. The passage did not always preserve the route a person used. He ran the comb over the map. Each remaining numbered tooth vibrated. At the empty seventh place, the map shivered and the drawn Latch turned sideways.

Ivara understood that Tovan had lied about borrowing the comb: it had not come from the collection. It had been waiting for this map. “What is the black thread?”

“An address before it learned to make a sound,” Tovan said. “If someone pulls it, the room may come back.”

“Or the room may unravel,” said Eren Vey.

Tovan looked at the two men. “You both know more than you say.”

Eren Vell took the thimble from the box. “I used to be called Pell Aven. The Hall erased my name when the Latch disappeared. I was its child.”

The name landed with a sound like a lock finding its slot. Ivara had heard the name Pell Aven in an old file: an apprentice missing from the Registry's winter roll. The porter was the same person, and he had carried the name Eren Vell for eight years. The Hall had not forgotten him; it had filed him under a job instead of a life.

Eren Vey stepped back from him. “You were the apprentice who stole the seventh tooth.”

“I found it in the false bottom,” Pell said. “Tovan had hidden it there. Before I sent you the box, I moved the tooth beneath the moth in the hidden chamber. The comb was breaking rooms into pieces, and I thought the Latch could keep them together.”

The truth explained the missing tooth and the false bottom. It did not explain the blue thread, or why the Latch had called to Ivara. She had never met Pell before he became Eren Vell. At least, that was what she believed.

The red key stayed cool. Tovan snatched the map from the basin, but the line between Hall and Orchard remained in the stone beneath it. A new door appeared in the Sump wall, small and round, with a brass lip.

From behind it, someone tapped three times.

Ivara put the glass thimble in her pocket. Pell kept the tin box. Eren Vey held the map, and Tovan held the bone comb. Four people, four objects, and an unopened door that knew the Mouth Room's note.

They did not open the round door. Tovan insisted that a door must be named before it was entered, but the small tapping continued from the other side. The sound was not impatient. It was counting their breaths.

Eren Vey asked Pell how he had come by the box. Pell said it had been on the lost-property shelf when he began work at the Hall, and that he had avoided it for years. On the day it was placed in Ivara's hands, it had appeared beside his porter cart. There was no record of a delivery. He had tied blue thread around it because the box's lid would not stay shut.

“Did you know what was inside?” Ivara asked.

“No. I knew who it was meant for.”

“Who?”

Pell looked at the crack in the door. “Someone who hears the note without asking what it means.”

Ivara could not tell whether he meant herself or the room. She took out the red key and laid it on the stone basin. The key did not warm or cool. Tovan said that was not an answer. Eren Vey replied that silence was often the only answer a room could give without being claimed.

The brass map showed no route from the Sump to the round door. Tovan reached for the map, then stopped. Eren turned it upside down. A cluster of marks appeared, all on the side where the false bottom had pressed against the paper. The marks formed a row of five seats, just as if the map were a place setting. He recognized the number because he had found five names in the hidden chamber once, in a childhood dream he thought was invented.

Pell watched him. “You remember the house?”

“I remember a song, but not the place.”

“Whose song?”

“I don't know.”

The black thread in the thimble quivered. Eren pulled his hand away. He said that the song had followed him to the Hall, but he had never heard it when anyone else was present. He had decided it was his mother's humming, though he had no proof. Now, in the Sump, the thread repeated the same three notes.

Tovan sat on the basin's rim. For the first time that day, he looked old. He told them the Registry was founded after a terrible confusion called the Great Partition, when rooms had been joined and divided without consent. The Hall's first examiners made the comb to separate each room's voice. The comb was a safeguard. It was also the instrument that caused the division.

“Which is why you kept the seventh tooth,” Ivara said.

“I did not keep it. I hid it where no one would use it.”

“Those are the same thing, if you have the only map.”

Tovan accepted that. He turned the bone comb over in his hands, and the numbered teeth made a faint dry chatter. The missing seventh tooth had been designed to listen for a room's first name, before it acquired all the names people later gave it. That was why it could both mend and undo the comb's work. The red key in Ivara's hand was a separate object: iron, forked, and warm with a lie. It was not a tooth from the bone comb.

Ivara looked again at the brass map. The line to the Latch did not lead down. It led through the Hall, past the Mouth Room, to the handprints on the new bark strip. She had seen the marks that morning, disobeyed Tovan, and turned the strip face up again. She realized the Hall had begun answering her, not the Registry.

The three taps came again, now from the inside of the box. Pell placed it on the floor. The lid lifted and settled three times. On the fourth, it stayed open. A second note, lower than the first, sounded from the round door. It was the answer to the Mouth Room's tone.

They moved away from the basin toward the surface passage, but remained in the Sump. Ivara tried to take the brass map, but it clung to the basin as if the stone had grown around its edges. Eren Vey said the map might be choosing its first bearer. He laid his palm beside it and described the childhood song he had heard. The map released with a soft click.

Pell watched him take it. “Does it feel familiar?”

“It feels like someone has remembered me.”

That was different from being recognized by the Hall. Eren put the map inside his coat. The glass thimble remained with Ivara, and the bone comb with Tovan. The box, still open, looked smaller now that the things hidden inside had been found.

Tovan asked Pell to describe the day the seventh tooth disappeared. Pell remembered the comb lying on the table in the first house. The children had been told not to touch it because the sound of the teeth could change a room. He had not understood the warning. Tovan had hidden the seventh tooth in the false bottom. Pell found it there, then moved it beneath the moth and tied it to the spool, hoping the comb would stop dividing the house. The comb did stop, but the room's names had already begun to separate.

“Why didn't you tell us?” Ivara asked.

“I tried to tell the Hall. The name I used was Pell Aven. Nobody found it in the files. When I told them I knew where the tooth was, they searched for a porter named Pell and found no one. Eventually I became the person they expected to see.”

The Hall had not literally changed his face. It had narrowed the ways he could introduce himself until one name was easier than all the others. Ivara could see how a mistaken record became a habit, and a habit became a life.

The round door tapped a fourth time. This time, the bone comb answered without Tovan touching it. Its unnumbered gap gave off a faint shimmer. He held the comb away from the box. The whole Sump floor tilted by a degree, and the clay addresses slid toward the stone basin.

Eren Vey caught the map before it fell. Its folded edges opened against his chest. Every line led to the chamber in the wall. Pell recognized the shape as the plan of the house before the division: one room with five thresholds, each threshold opening wherever a person needed to enter.

“Not a door,” said Tovan. “A choice.”

The distinction stayed with Ivara. She looked at the round outline and waited. The tapping stopped. Nobody had opened it, and still the room had let them hear its answer. They were learning to approach without forcing an entrance.
